## 6. PHASE TWO — BUILD A PROFESSIONALLY MODELED BASE CHARACTER

* Naturally proportioned shoulders.
* A lean chest.
* Defined waist.
* Believable pelvis and hips.
* Slender arms.
* Naturally shaped elbows.
* Proper wrist transitions.
* Proportional hands and fingers.
* Lean thighs.
* Defined knees.
* Tapered calves.
* Natural ankles.
* Properly sized feet.

Preserve the illustrated character's stylization.
Do not exaggerate muscle mass.

**Geometry quality requirements.** Use professional mesh construction techniques appropriate to the task.
Avoid visible primitive-based final anatomy.
Do not assemble the character from overlapping cylinders, spheres, cubes, and capsules.
Temporary primitive blockouts are permitted, but they cannot be treated as finished anatomy.
Prefer a suitable, editable humanoid base mesh with clean topology.
If an appropriate existing mesh is available and properly licensed for commercial use, it may be adapted.
Do not download, purchase, or incorporate third-party assets without verifying their availability and license.

**Required initial Blender validation.** After constructing the body:

1. Position the character in a neutral modeling pose.
2. Create a front orthographic camera.
3. Create a side orthographic camera.
4. Render both views.
5. Inspect the renders.
6. Compare proportions against the original turnaround.
7. Correct the geometry where necessary.

## 11. PHASE SEVEN — CONTINUOUS RENDER, INSPECT, CORRECT
This is the most important difference between this assignment and ordinary Blender scripting.
You are connected to Blender, so you must use an iterative production process.
For every major character component:

1. Model or modify the geometry in Blender.
2. Render the relevant view.
3. Retrieve the rendered output using your available tools.
4. Inspect the actual image.
5. Compare it with the corresponding original turnaround view.
6. Identify the largest visible discrepancy.
7. Correct that discrepancy in Blender.
8. Render again.
9. Confirm whether the change improved the result.
10. Continue until the component meets the best achievable reference fidelity.

Never assume that successfully generated geometry is visually correct.
Do not declare a phase complete without inspecting its rendered output when render inspection is available.

**Establish a consistent comparison environment.** Use:
